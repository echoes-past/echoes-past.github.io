# For Educators

## Unit Overview

This curricular supplement is designed to provide teachers and students with immediate, local connections to history using stories about a place known throughout time by several names: Tk̓úpa, the Utilla Indian Agency, Fort Henrietta, and the City of Echo, Oregon. The lessons in this unit have been designed to fit the 2024 Oregon Social Science Standards for Grade 4, but could easily be scaled to Grades 3, 6, or 8 if desired.

The content of this unit has been designed in alignment with [Átaw Ititámat (the Educational Values)](https://ctuir.org/departments/education/) outlined by The Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation's Education Program and with consideration for the [College, Career, and Civic Life (C3) Framework](https://www.socialstudies.org/standards/c3) for Social Studies State Standards developed by the [National Council for the Social Studies](https://www.socialstudies.org/).

## Enduring Understandings

For thousands of years and continuing into the present, members of the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation (the Cayuse, Umatilla, and Walla Walla tribes) have studied, cared for, honored, been shaped by, and thrived along the Umatilla River. This is particularly true at specific named places. *See also [Essential Understandings of Native Americans in Oregon](https://www.oregon.gov/ode/students-and-family/equity/NativeAmericanEducation/Documents/SB13%20Curriculum/CLEAN%20Essential%20Understandings%20of%20Native%20Americans%20in%20Oregon%20June%202020%20(002).pdf).*

There are many ways to understand and connect with the place or the environment where we live and through which we travel. Maps, place names, and archaeological investigations are different but related ways for people to understand aspects of a place or environment.

The arrival of various new peoples to a place brings change. These changes may include what places are called, how they are used, and the kinds of relationships people and other beings have there.

Changes brought by newcomers are not linear or universal. Many different peoples' relationships with the place and some aspects of the place itself remain unchanged.

The study of the past through history and archaeology explores the push and pull of change and continuity throughout time.

## Learning Goals

Throughout this unit, students will…

- … apply terms related to the field of archaeology in their discussions of the past.
- … investigate how larger trends in state and national history directly connect to their local communities.
- … internalize essential understandings regarding Indigenous Peoples in Oregon, particularly the Walla Walla, Cayuse, and Umatilla peoples.

**Description:** 

This project is a unique synthesis of contracted work and individual student research bridging the traditional disciplines of public history, anthropology, education, pedagogy, and museum curation. This project is contracted with the City of Echo and is dictated by a Memorandum of Agreement between the U.S. Army Corps, the Oregon State Historic Preservation Office, and the City of Echo based on cultural resource remediation requirements negotiated by the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation (CTUIR). 

The task pertinent to the website I’m hoping to develop is outlined as follows:

TASK 2\. *Educational curriculum and/or traveling exhibit.* 

Within two years of execution of the MOA, the City will create a curriculum and/or traveling exhibit for use at Nixyáawii Community School, Pendleton School District, and Echo School District. The curriculum and/or traveling exhibit will be developed by the City and the Echo Historical Museum in consultation with the CTUIR Education Department (if a curriculum is developed), the Tamástslikt Cultural Institute (if a traveling exhibit is being created), and additional consultants as needed. **\[*We fall under the “additional consultants” umbrella.*\]** The consulting parties will have an opportunity to comment on the text of the draft curriculum and those comments will be addressed. The final curriculum and/or traveling exhibit text will also be presented at an Intergovernmental Cultural Resource Council (ICRC) meeting and/or published as a journal article in the Journal of Northwest Anthropology or similar publication.

I have developed a supplementary unit meant to add local specificity and nuance to the Dept. of Education requirements for teaching state and local history during the 4th grade. The unit is called “Echoes of the Past” and consists of five place-based and hands-on lessons delivered in 7 or more blocks of time. The curriculum is still being workshopped with local teachers to ensure its long-term utility and success, and a working draft can be found [HERE](https://vandalsuidaho-my.sharepoint.com/:w:/g/personal/mcaves_uidaho_edu/IQC1BGohsO7xRoT5O7ptVfYKAS4HZKW0GkHes2OxLBBrKpA?e=ikb0xv) for your reference. All of the materials will soon be migrated to a Google Drive account \[[echoes.past.oregon@gmail.com](mailto:echoes.past.oregon@gmail.com)\] to ensure compatibility with the Google Classroom system that Oregon schools use. 

The website will primarily function as a landing page for the curriculum and a more visually engaging and accessible way for teachers to get to each of the appropriate Google Drive links for each lesson. Based on conversations with cultural resource and education specialists from the CTUIR, the incorporation of the Sahaptian/Umatilla language throughout the unit is of vital importance to accurately communicating about the place throughout time and highlighting indigenous ways of knowing. The language inclusion presents a potential barrier to both educators and students who are not only learning new information but also part of a new language. To ensure the accessibility and improve the likelihood that teachers and students engage with this vital aspect of the curriculum, I have worked with a master speaker of the Umatilla language to record audio files with accurate pronunciations of the vocabulary words and key phrases included throughout the lessons. This “interactive glossary” as I have termed it, will be built as one of the pages in the site for students and teachers to learn how to pronounce these words and hopefully feel more able to engage with the language throughout the unit.

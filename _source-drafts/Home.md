# **How this curriculum came to be…**  You may remember the severe flooding of the Umatilla River in 2020, and the damage that it caused throughout communities in the county of the same name. These floods not only threatened roads, cattle pastures, cropland, and homes, but also a very important archaeological site in Echo, Oregon. 

\[EchoFlood.jpg\] \[FloodErosion.jpg\]

Flood remediation work in 2023 further posed risks to the site and limited future opportunities to study this significant site. To address concerns raised by the Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation and the Oregon State Heritage Preservation Office, the City of Echo began working with historical archaeologists from the University of Idaho in 2024, to develop supplementary curricular material for local schools about this unique place. 

\[FloodRestoration.jpg\]

At that time, Meghan Caves was just starting her doctoral studies in historical archaeology and public history. She took on the task of reanalyzing all of the artifacts previously excavated in the 1980s and 1990s and working with local educators and historians to develop the curriculum. Her previous work at the Umatilla National Forest and investment in making history accessible to all audiences strongly influenced her investment in the place-based and hands-on approaches employed throughout this unit.

# AI Use Disclosure

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) are now ubiquitous forces in daily life. Leveraging these tools to make content more accessible and engaging for students is a valuable asset to support the goals of this curriculum. However, it is essential to be mindful and transparent about the use of these tools to reduce their part in ongoing colonial harm. Please see [this article about Indigenous Data Sovereignty](https://www.iwgia.org/en/articles/iw-2026-indigenous-data-sovereignty?lang=en) for a brief overview of these issues.

[Vocova](https://vocova.app/about) was used to ensure all background information for educators is accessible by generating transcripts for any audio resources that did not already include transcriptions. Their company page states that they do not “sell user transcripts, train external models on user content, or share recordings with third parties for analytics.” Based on this language, the content of this publicly available podcast was more than likely used to train Vocova’s *internal* AI model.

Closed captions for all of the videos used throughout the curriculum were generated using the Speech-to-Text functionality within Adobe Premiere. The Adobe software suite is [General Data Protection Regulation](https://gdpr.eu) compliant. For all versions of the Premiere software after 2023, audio and transcription data are rendered on the user’s device and uses a static non-server-based transcription engine within the software on the user’s device. None of these projects were uploaded to the Adobe Cloud and none of the content has been or will be shared through the Adobe Stock platform at any point. See Section 2.2 of [Adobe’s General Terms of Use](https://www.adobe.com/legal/terms.html) for specifics on the company’s access and usage of user content.
